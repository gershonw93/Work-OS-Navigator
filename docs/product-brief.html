The short version

One place for the job, from the estimate to the last payment

A GC runs a job out of a dozen places at once - a spreadsheet for the budget, a text thread with the plumber, an inbox full of certificates, and a bookkeeper asking what got billed. SyteNav is one place for all of it, built for the person standing on the site rather than the person reporting on it afterwards.

Every job opens on an Overview: what you have received from the client, what you have committed to vendors, what is waiting on you, and what is waiting on somebody else. Not a checklist - a GC knows how to run a job. What nobody can know without opening six tabs is what has piled up since they last looked.

How a job moves

Four stages, in the order they actually happen

  1. 01

    Price it

    Build or upload an estimate and AI reads it into line items - including your payment terms, so "50% deposit, 40% at rough-in" becomes figures you can actually request later. Send it as a proposal; accept it and it becomes a live job.

    Estimate · Budget

  2. 02

    Buy it out

    Send a scope to subs and get priced quotes back. Inviting one emails them the request there and then; a second email to somebody who has not answered is a reminder and says so. They need no account and no password - a private link opens the scope, the plans and a form on a phone. Compare line by line, award one, and the subcontract, its payment schedule and its budget line all exist. The package you pick - labour and material, labour only, material only - decides who supplies the material, so a request cannot go out contradicting itself. A quote carrying a valid-until date is flagged before you award it, so nobody signs a price the vendor stopped holding last month. Insurance and licences are chased from the same place, and compliance goes by the DATE on the document rather than a status somebody remembered to set: a live certificate is current, one inside thirty days flags amber, and a lapsed one goes red the day it starts to matter.

    A sub with a question about the scope can message you from the same link, once they make a free account - a name and a password on that page, nothing to confirm by email - a vendor account that sees only the jobs and requests they are invited to and cannot create jobs of its own. Each sub has their own conversation with you, so one bidder never sees what you told another, and your answer reaches them by email. Quoting itself still needs no account.

    Quotes & Bids · Subs & Team · Compliance

  3. 03

    Build it

    Plans with pins, a schedule, tasks on a board, daily logs with photos, a time clock, permits, inspections, RFIs and submittals. Field-first: everything here works on a phone with one hand and a glove on the other.

    Every job can carry the list of inspections it will need, decided up front - footing to final, plus your own types - and each line says where it stands until it passes. A lot with no city sewer or water suggests the septic and well inspections that go with it. Inspections keep two dates apart on purpose: the date the field needs it, and the date the jurisdiction actually gave you. Only the second one reaches the calendar, so nothing sits in your team's Outlook as a confirmed appointment that nobody has arranged.

    The time clock takes a live selfie and checks the punch against where the job actually is - inside 250m and it passes, outside and it is flagged for somebody to look at. Every job has one point on the map for that, taken from its address; where the address puts the pin in the wrong place - a site entrance on a different road, a long driveway, new construction with no street number yet - you can set it by hand, or just stand on the site and press Use my current location. If a job has no usable point, SyteNav says so on the Time Clock tab and records the punch unchecked. It never invents one: a pin in the wrong place would flag an honest worker standing exactly where they should be.

    Once awarded, a sub sees the job's schedule with their own work marked, submits RFIs and bills, and can report that they are running late. A report moves nothing: it lands on your schedule with their days and reason filled into your usual delay screen, so you see which trades it would push before you apply it, and they hear back either way.

    Plans · Schedule · Tasks · Daily Logs · Time Clock · Permits · Inspections · RFIs

  4. 04

    Get paid

    Approve the bills your subs send you, bill your client from costs already approved with your markup applied, record what comes in, and watch the fee you have actually earned rather than a flat percentage of a guess.

    Bills from subs · Billing the client · Pay Apps · Change Orders

The money

Two billing styles, because residential and commercial are not the same job

Simple invoicing

For residential and cost-plus work. Invoices are built from costs you have already approved - sub bills and material receipts - each carrying its own markup, so nothing is retyped and the figures cannot drift.

AIA progress billing

For commercial and bank-funded work. The G702/G703 application is the bill you send, with schedule of values, retainage and stored materials handled properly.

Deposits and stage payments

A deposit is money taken before there are any costs to bill against, so it is not built like an invoice. Your estimate's payment terms appear already priced - press once to request the deposit, and it goes to the client by email and appears on their portal. Ask in dollars or as a percentage; either way it shows what the figure comes to before you send it.

Your fee is earned, not assumed

On a cost-plus job the contractor's fee is calculated from the cost that has actually been billed, line by line - including lines you agreed to pass through at cost, and lines negotiated at a different rate. A flat "rate × spend" is close but wrong, and it is wrong in the direction that costs you money.

What it costs

Your first 15 days are free. SyteNav is invite-only: once you are approved you get the whole product on a real job for 15 days, with no card taken and nothing to cancel. After that you pick one of the plans above. Anybody who was in the beta before billing existed keeps free access - we have not put a clock on people who were here first.

Finished jobs do not count

Only active projects use your plan - jobs being planned, running, or on hold. Complete or cancel a job and it stops counting while staying fully readable forever: the budget, invoices, daily logs, photos, documents and Job History all stay where they are. Reaching the limit means moving up a plan or closing out a job that is done, and neither deletes anything. Settings → Billing shows the count as it stands.

In a development, every house or unit is a job and counts; the development holding them is a folder and does not. A batch that would go past your plan is refused as a whole, never half created.

Your team, your subs and your clients are all free

Team members are unlimited on every plan. Subs and clients work through links rather than logins, so there is no seat to buy for them - which matters more than it sounds: where the field is charged per seat, seats get rationed, and rationed seats mean the people on site are not in the system. A sub who wants to message you about a quote makes a free account for it; that is never charged either.

Paying annually

Ten months charged for twelve months of service - so $99 a month becomes $990 a year, and the middle plan $2,990. Monthly plans cancel anytime.

What an AI scan is

One document read by the AI: a quote, a sub bill, a receipt, an inspector's card, a permit, a submittal or a compliance document. A read that fails never counts against you - you only spend a scan when you get an answer back. The count runs by calendar month and refills on the 1st. If you run out, scanning pauses until then or until you move up a plan, and everything else in SyteNav carries on: the limit is on having documents read for you, not on running the job.

The client's side

One link, no account, nothing they can break

Every job has a private client link. It is read-only, works on a phone, and needs no sign-up. They see progress, the schedule, permits, recent daily logs, any selections still waiting on them, invoices you have sent, payments you have requested, and a record of what they have already paid.

Selections chase themselves. Each one shows where it stands without anybody setting it - waiting on the client once their options are on the link, chosen, ordered, and installed when the schedule line for that work reaches 100%. Anything still waiting on them has a Remind client button that emails the item, the options and the date you need it by, with one tap to their selections page, and the row shows when they were last reminded.

Nothing appears there until you have actually sent it - a draft invoice or an unsent payment request stays yours. And the moment money is recorded, their page says so: the request disappears and the payment is acknowledged. "Did they even get my money?" is the one question a client page must never leave open.

Who can hand that link out is its own permission, set per role or per person. It is on by default for admins, managers, project managers and office staff, and off for field supervisors, workers and any subcontractor you invite onto the job - the portal carries your invoices to your client, so it is not something the crew or a vendor should be able to open. Replacing a link, which cuts off a client using the old one, is a separate permission again.

Bookkeeping

QuickBooks, answered the way a bookkeeper would ask

Does it sync with QuickBooks Online?
Yes. One-way, SyteNav into QuickBooks, so your bookkeeper is not re-entering anything. Once connected it runs itself - there is no button to remember.
What actually goes across?
In SyteNavIn QuickBooksWhen
CustomersCustomerAs you add or edit them
Subs & suppliersVendorFirst time you approve their bill
Bills from subsBillThe moment you approve it
Paying a sub's billBill Payment, applied to that billThe moment you mark it paid

Voiding a sent invoiceThe invoice is voided there tooAs you void it
Will an unpaid invoice show as money owed?
Yes - that is the point. Send it and it lands in QuickBooks as a receivable with your own invoice number, the due date and the job name on it, so you get proper aging rather than only seeing cash when it arrives.
Which invoice does a payment settle?
The ones you recorded it against, by the amounts you gave - not "the oldest one still open". A part payment leaves the invoice part paid, with the balance still owed on both sides. One cheque covering several invoices goes across as ONE QuickBooks payment applied to each of them, so it matches the single deposit on the bank statement. Only money that settles nothing in particular - a deposit taken before there is an invoice - books as a sales receipt instead.
One check covering several houses in a development?
Record it once on the development and split it house by house - against each house's invoices, as a deposit or draw on a house, or both. Every house shows its own share. When the whole check pays invoices billed to one customer, it goes to QuickBooks as one payment applied across all of them, matching the single deposit; otherwise each house's share goes across separately, all carrying the same check number.
What about overpayments?
Held as credit on account if you say so when you record the payment. In QuickBooks it is unapplied credit on the customer, on the same payment; applying it to a later invoice updates that payment rather than adding a second one, so the money is never counted twice.
Does it record how the client paid?
Yes. Check, ACH, wire, cash or card carries through to the QuickBooks record - the field your bookkeeper reconciles a bank statement against. QuickBooks only knows a few of those out of the box; the rest are created in your file the first time you use them.
Can I tell which job a QuickBooks record belongs to?
Every record carries the job name in its memo and a reference number you can search for, so a customer with three jobs does not turn into three unidentifiable receipts.
What if QuickBooks is down when I record something?
Your record saves regardless - the payment is the work, QuickBooks is a side effect. The miss is logged and swept up by the next sync, and the settings screen tells you plainly how many records are waiting. That includes voids: if a void does not get through, the invoice says so rather than showing a tick, because an invoice that is still an open receivable over there is exactly the thing you need to know about.
I have more than one company. Do they share books?
No. Each company connects to its own QuickBooks file and only ever pushes there. That is deliberate - separate businesses must never write into each other's books.

Straight answers

What it does not do yet

Worth knowing before you ask it to do something it cannot. None of these lose data - the information is still in SyteNav; they are places a person still does the last step.

  • Booking appointments SyteNav does not book anything with anybody on your behalf - it does not call, text or email an inspector, a township or a client to set a time. It records the time once a person has arranged it, and confirmed dates appear on the calendar and in the feed you can add to Google, Apple or Outlook.
  • Text messages SyteNav sends email and in-app notifications. It does not send SMS.
  • Booking an inspection SyteNav does not contact the inspector or the township. It tells the right person at your company that an inspection is needed, hands them the numbers already on the job - the issuing authority and inspector from your permits, plus any inspectors in your Directory - and then records what they were given: the confirmed date, who they spoke to and the confirmation number. The phone call is still a person's job.
  • Placing a job on the map A job's map point comes from its address, and an address a map service cannot resolve - a street with no town or ZIP, or a placeholder typed in a hurry - leaves the job unplaced rather than guessed at. A list of addresses is checked on the map before any job is made, and a house the map could only find the street for is created without a pin rather than given the middle of the street. SyteNav says which jobs those are and lets you set the point by hand, but it will not invent one, and it cannot tell a placeholder that happens to name a real town from a genuine address.
  • QuickBooks · one direction Nothing recorded directly in QuickBooks comes back into SyteNav. Edit something over there and the two will disagree until it is fixed here.
  • Credit is per job An overpayment, or a payment made ahead, can be held as the client's credit on account and applied to a later invoice. That credit belongs to the job it was paid on - it cannot be spent on the same client's other job - and there is no refund button for it yet.
  • Editing a sent invoice Deliberately not possible - editing a document your client is holding leaves your copy and theirs disagreeing. Void it and raise a corrected one instead; voiding sends the void to QuickBooks and releases its costs to be billed again.
  • Signing up · no self-serve start There is still no button a stranger can press to start the free 15 days, and no public demo you can click into. Access is by invite, so the pricing page asks for your details rather than your card, and the trial begins once you are approved. The page says that rather than offering a button that opens something else.
  • House models · applied, not linked A model's plans, budget, inspections and punch items are copied onto each house when you apply it. Changing the model changes no house until you apply again - that adds only what is new, and can update a budget amount the template has changed on the houses still on the old figure, while a figure somebody changed on one house is left alone. Taking something off the model does not take it off houses that already have it.
  • Claude · adds and edits, never deletes, pays, approves or sends If you allow it, Claude can add and change records right across SyteNav and file documents you hand it. It cannot delete anything, record a payment, approve a bill or a change order, send anything to a client or a sub, or change a markup, a payment schedule or a vendor's details - a person still does those in SyteNav. It reads what a document says but often cannot pass SyteNav the file itself, so it may hand you a link to drop the original on. Roles that only see the jobs they are assigned to cannot connect it yet.
  • Sub-side experience Subcontractors can quote, message you about a quote request, see the schedule, report a delay on their own work for you to approve, submit RFIs and bills, and see their own jobs - but their side is thinner than the GC's. It is built for the contractor first.

Everything else

The parts that are easy to miss

Email that actually sends

Quote requests, document requests, shared files, client invoices and payment requests all go out from SyteNav - properly branded, from your domain, with the link the recipient needs. If sending is not set up or fails, it says so and hands you the link to send yourself rather than showing a green tick over an email that never left.

You choose what you are told about, and so does the company

Each person has their own switches for the bell and for email. Email is off by default for most, on for the six where finding out at your next login is too late - somebody waiting on your sign-off, a bill needing approval, an insurance certificate about to lapse, or a package you bid on being decided. Separately, an admin sets who gets told at all: which roles or people hear about an inspection, a change order, a bill waiting for approval.

Every file in one place

Files holds your company documents and, under Job Files, everything on your jobs - plans, site photos, quotes, submittals and sub bills - grouped by job. Each person sees only the kinds of file their role can already open on the job.

Reports you can save, share and print

Reports builds a report on one job or many, on everything for one customer or developer, or on a sub or a supplier across every job they are on. Pick a period - this month, last quarter, your own dates - and tick what goes in it: the money summary, subcontracts and change orders, bills, material purchases, budget against actual, margin, client invoices and payments, the schedule, permits, inspections, daily logs and hours. Save it and it runs again with fresh numbers whenever you open it; save it as a PDF; or share it as a link - emailed for you if you like - that the developer or the sub opens with no account.

A shared report is a copy as it read when you sent it, not a live window onto the job, and you can turn the link off. A report about a sub or a supplier only ever carries their own rows - never another vendor's, and never your client billing or margin - which is what makes it safe to send them. Each section also follows your permissions: somebody who cannot see margin cannot put it in a report.

Ask Claude about your jobs - and let it do the typing

Add SyteNav to Claude as a custom connector - paste one address, sign in to SyteNav, press Allow - and you can ask it "what is overdue on the Henderson job?", "which inspections still need booking?" or "what do we still owe subs on Lot 14?" - it reads the answer out of SyteNav. It works in Claude on the web, the desktop app and Claude Code, with no setup file, and it uses your own permissions: it sees exactly what you can see, only on jobs your company runs.

Tick "also let it add records" and it does the typing too, anywhere in the app: new jobs and customers, budget lines, subcontracts, schedule items and date changes, tasks and task notes, daily logs, bills from subs, pay applications, quote requests, permits, inspection bookings and results, RFIs, submittal reviews, selections, change orders, compliance documents, material receipts, equipment and contacts. Drop a document into the chat - a sub's bill, an insurance certificate, a permit, a receipt - and it works out what it is, which job and whose, files it in the right place, and asks you first if any of that is unclear. Anything a client, a sub or QuickBooks would see waits for you: a bill waits for approval; invoices, payment requests, pay applications and quote requests stay unsent drafts; a daily log waits for review; and moving a date shows you who else moves and asks before any sub is emailed. It never deletes, approves or records payments; asking twice does not enter twice; and everything it does is marked "via Claude" in Job History. Each connection on Settings → Integrations says whether it can add, can be switched either way, and can be revoked on its own.

Equipment, including what is broken

Every tool and machine, who has it and which job it is on, checked out and back in from a phone. Anybody can mark something as needing repair wherever it is, or as it comes back, and the problem shows on the list for the next person who reaches for it. Send it away to be fixed, mark it repaired or retire it, and each step lands in its history with who did it.

Developments: multi-unit work and groups of houses

A building or a street can be a development holding a job per unit, with budget and progress rolling up across all of them - so twelve townhouses are twelve jobs you can actually run, not one job with twelve of everything in it. A development grows and changes as the work does: add one more house to it (with its model applied), move jobs you already have into it, move a job between developments, or make a new development out of jobs started one at a time.

Scattered lots come in from a list: a spreadsheet, a PDF or a photo of your lot schedule, read into rows you check before anything is created - each address looked up on the map, with its lot and block, parcel, lot size, city water and sewer, garage side and any environmental note carried onto the job. Every job can hold those lot details, not only the ones made from a list.

Houses built to the same design share a model: its plans (with separate left- and right-garage sets where they differ), its budget, its required inspections and its punch list - each punch item with who does it, a teammate, a sub or any name - are set up once and applied to the houses you pick, after a preview of what each one will get. Budget templates are edited line by line - add a line, change an amount - and applying the model again carries the change to the houses you tick. The development keeps its own main budget for land, roads and sitework, added to the houses' budgets in the roll-up.
